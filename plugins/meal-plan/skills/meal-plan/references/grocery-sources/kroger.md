# Kroger Product Reference

This file is the store-specific product catalog for Kroger. It lists products organized by store section with real brand names currently available at most Kroger locations. Each section opens with weekly quantity guidance scaled to a typical 2,500-calorie-per-day plan and ends with a "skip" list explaining what to avoid and why.

The guiding principle across all Kroger shopping, regardless of diet type, is to flip every package before putting it in the cart and check the sugar content per serving. Most diet types (diabetic, weight loss, heart-healthy) benefit from avoiding anything with more than 3 grams of added sugar per serving on meat, sauces, marinades, or prepared items.

## Cheese

Weekly quantity guidance for most meal plans: pick two cheeses totaling 600 to 700 grams (20 to 24 ounces). One hard cheese for lunch boxes and snacking, and one soft or interesting cheese for variety. Add string cheese or Babybel if the person wants portable snacks.

### Hard and aged cheeses

Sharp cheddar is the workhorse. Buy it as a block (Kroger store brand or Cracker Barrel) and cube it yourself, or grab pre-cubed if convenience matters more than price. Parmesan or Asiago wedges work well as shavings on salads. Gouda, especially the smoked variety, holds up well in charcuterie boxes. Manchego gives a subtle sheep-milk tang. Swiss or Jarlsberg offer mild nutty flavors. Pepper Jack brings heat.

### Soft and spreadable cheeses

Brie pairs beautifully with berries or cured meats. Boursin with garlic and herbs spreads onto cucumber slices and bell pepper strips. Goat cheese logs crumble onto salads. Full-fat Philadelphia cream cheese works as a base for dips or eaten with smoked salmon.

### Grab-and-go cheese formats

Babybel is the ultimate pocket cheese, durable enough for a truck glove box or a work bag. String cheese (Sargento or Frigo) holds up in a lunch cooler. Pre-cubed cheese packs save prep time. Good Culture 4% cottage cheese is a high-protein breakfast base. Whole milk ricotta can be spooned with berries as a near-dessert.

### Skip these cheeses

Fat-free anything tastes worse and often adds sugar to compensate. American cheese singles are ultra-processed and barely qualify as cheese. Cheese spreads in tubs often have added sugar. Sargento Balanced Breaks that include dried fruit are sugar traps despite being marketed as "balanced."

## Meat and Protein

Weekly quantity guidance: plan on about 6 to 7 pounds of raw meat total for the week at roughly 2,500 calories per day. Split across 2 to 3 chicken meals, 1 sausage meal, 1 to 2 red meat meals, and 1 to 2 wing nights. One rotisserie chicken in the mix stretches further than expected.

### Frozen chicken in the Tyson style

Tyson Any'tizers are reliable if you stick to plain, Buffalo, or hot wings. Perdue Wings and Foster Farms Take Out Crispy Wings work the same way. For quicker meals, Tyson Grilled and Ready Chicken Breast Strips reheat in the air fryer in five minutes. Perdue Short Cuts Grilled Chicken Strips are refrigerated and need no cooking at all. Just Bare Grilled Chicken Breast Strips have cleaner ingredients for those who read labels.

### Raw chicken, better value and texture

Boneless skinless chicken thighs are the most forgiving cut for new cooks. They stay juicy in the air fryer at 400 degrees for 16 to 18 minutes, and they take any seasoning well. Bone-in thighs give crispy skin at 22 to 25 minutes. Drumsticks run cheap and cook in 20 to 22 minutes. Leg quarters are the cheapest cut per pound. Raw party wings (whole wings, not the pre-separated drumettes and flats) come out crispier than frozen Tyson wings and cost less per pound.

### Sausage, checking for sugar under 2 grams per link

Johnsonville Brats work well in their original, beer, and cheddar varieties. Johnsonville Italian sausage (both hot and "sweet," where sweet refers to the pepper type rather than added sugar) is reliable. Aidells Italian Style with Mozzarella and Aidells Andouille are both safe. Eckrich Polska Kielbasa, Hillshire Farm Smoked Sausage in beef or turkey variants, and Premio Italian Sausage all work. Al Fresco Chicken Sausage is safe in Italian, Spicy, or Roasted Garlic varieties.

### Beef

Sirloin steak tips or pre-cubed steak bites cook fast (7 to 9 minutes in the air fryer). Ribeye or New York strip steaks run 8 to 10 minutes. Pre-cubed beef kabob meat works the same. Ground beef at 93/7 can be made into patties and frozen in advance. Boneless thin-cut short ribs take 12 to 15 minutes. Pre-marinated carne asada is fine as long as the sugar content stays under 3 grams. Rosina or Cooked Perfect Homestyle meatballs work for quick Italian-style dinners.

### Pork

Thin-cut boneless pork chops cook in 10 to 12 minutes. Bone-in pork chops at 12 minutes. Bacon in the air fryer takes 8 to 10 minutes and comes out crispier than a pan.

### Deli counter options

Sliced turkey (Boar's Head or Kroger's low-sodium option), sliced ham, roast beef, salami, prosciutto, sopressata, and capicola are all safe at the deli counter. Rotisserie chicken at 6 to 7 dollars per bird stretches across three meals. Deli grilled chicken breasts work as protein base for salads.

### Skip these proteins

Any frozen chicken labeled Honey BBQ, Teriyaki, Sweet and Spicy, Bourbon, Orange, Sesame, or Sweet Anything. These are candy in meat form. Tyson Crispy Strips and breaded chicken products spike blood sugar through the breading even if the meat is fine. Maple, apple, or breakfast sausage unless the sugar content is under 2 grams. Aidells Chicken and Apple sausage specifically has added sugar and should be skipped.

## Produce

Weekly quantity guidance: enough greens, vegetables, and limited fruit to support five to seven dinner salads and a few pieces of fruit paired with protein or fat across the week.

### Low-impact fruits

All berries (strawberries, blueberries, raspberries, blackberries) are the gold standard for diet types requiring blood sugar management. Avocado technically qualifies as a fruit and has essentially zero impact on blood sugar. Olives behave the same way (technically a fruit, low carb).

### Moderate-impact fruits

Apples are better small and always eaten with cheese or peanut butter. Pears behave similarly. Oranges, clementines, and tangerines are fine in one-piece portions. Peaches, plums, and nectarines work. Kiwi adds variety. Cherries as a small handful.

### High-impact fruits (use caution with diabetic or weight-loss plans)

Bananas spike harder than most people expect. Split one in half rather than eating a whole one, and time it before physical activity. Grapes are essentially candy on a stem and should be limited to a small handful. Pineapple and mango cause bigger spikes. Watermelon is surprisingly rough on blood sugar despite its reputation as a "light" fruit.

### Vegetables

Bagged spring mix, bagged romaine, cherry tomatoes, cucumbers, bell peppers (the 3-pack is the best value), avocados, lemons. Steamable broccoli in the freezer aisle is the fastest side dish available.

### Skip these fruits entirely (for blood sugar management)

Dried fruit concentrates the sugar roughly four times compared to fresh, so raisins, craisins, and dates should stay out of the cart. Fruit juice is liquid sugar with no fiber to slow absorption. Canned fruit in syrup is obvious. Fruit cups even in juice still spike hard.

## Prepared Meals

Weekly quantity guidance: keep two to three backup prepared items on hand for days when even 20 minutes of cooking feels like too much. The rule is to flip the box and check net carbs (total carbs minus fiber). Aim for under 20 grams of net carbs for dinner, under 15 for lunch, for most diet types.

### Refrigerated heat-and-eat

Home Chef rotisserie chicken from the Kroger deli is the best value in the store. Home Chef also sells heat-and-eat meatloaf, pot roast, and pulled pork; check carb counts individually. Stouffer's Fit Kitchen bowls vary in quality. Kroger deli chicken salad, egg salad, and tuna salad work if they do not have added sugar. Deli deviled eggs and pre-peeled hard-boiled eggs (convenient but overpriced).

### Frozen meals that genuinely work for restricted diets

Real Good Foods is the standout brand for low-carb frozen meals. Their enchiladas, bowls, and breakfast sandwiches typically clock in at 4 to 10 grams of net carbs. Atkins frozen meals are reliably low carb. Healthy Choice Power Bowls in the chicken and vegetable varieties land at 20 to 30 grams of carbs, acceptable for active days. Kroger Simple Truth Cauliflower Crust pizza, topped with meat and cheese, makes a credible dinner.

### Prepared sides worth grabbing

Greek salad from the deli (pick out the croutons), chicken Caesar salad (skip the croutons), antipasto trays (essentially ready-made charcuterie with meat, cheese, and olives).

### Skip these prepared meals

Lean Cuisine, Smart Ones, and Weight Watchers meals are low-calorie but heavy on pasta and rice. Amy's Organic bowls, despite the "healthy" marketing, are mostly brown rice and beans. Sweet Earth has the same issue. Any meal described as "Mediterranean" or "Asian" usually has rice or noodles as the base.

## Pantry and Seasonings

Weekly quantity guidance: these are one-time setup purchases that last four to eight weeks. If the person's pantry is already stocked, skip this section on their shopping list.

Extra virgin olive oil is the main cooking and dressing fat. Avocado oil has a higher smoke point for air fryer use. Salt, black pepper, garlic powder, and onion powder cover most seasoning needs. One jar of Slap Ya Mama or Tony Chachere's Creole seasoning handles 90 percent of meat preparations. Italian seasoning blend adds variety. Paprika and smoked paprika round out the spice rack.

For condiments: Frank's RedHot or Crystal hot sauce, spicy brown mustard or Dijon, red wine vinegar or apple cider vinegar for salads. Kalamata olives, green olives, pepperoncini or banana peppers, and dill pickles (no-sugar-added) for charcuterie boxes.

For snacks and backup protein: unsalted almonds, walnuts, and pecans. Natural peanut butter with only peanuts and salt in the ingredients. StarKist or Kroger tuna pouches and canned chicken for emergency protein.

## Drinks

This section is particularly important for diabetic and weight-loss diet types, where liquid sugar is the single biggest lever.

### Drinks to enjoy freely

Water, aiming for 80 or more ounces per day (more on active days). Sparkling water like LaCroix, Bubly, or Kroger store brand. Black coffee. Unsweetened iced tea. Zero-sugar electrolyte packets like LMNT or Liquid IV Sugar Free, especially useful on hot and physically demanding days.

### Drinks to skip

Regular soda, at 40 or more grams of sugar per can. Sweet tea. Orange juice, apple juice, and any fruit juice regardless of marketing. Regular Gatorade or Powerade. Sweetened coffee creamers. Vitamin Water and Snapple. Beer works against both blood sugar management and weight loss; a one or two drink maximum if the person drinks at all.

## Dairy

Eggs are the workhorse ingredient for low-carb and diabetic plans. Buy 18 or 24-count depending on weekly consumption. Butter (unsalted or salted, either works). Fage 5% Greek yogurt for breakfast bowls (32 oz tub). Good Culture 4% cottage cheese (16 oz tub). Heavy cream for coffee or sauces if desired.

## Frozen

Steamable broccoli bags are essential for fast sides. Frozen mixed berries as backup if fresh spoils. Frozen cauliflower rice for low-carb base dishes. Frozen spinach for quick protein-and-greens meals.

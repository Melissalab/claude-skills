# Diabetic Diet Type

This file captures everything specific to planning meals for someone managing Type 2 diabetes or high A1C through diet. It covers the calorie and macronutrient logic, the handful of key habits worth emphasizing in the document's quick-start section, and a complete recipe library built around low-sugar, low-carb, high-protein meals that fit into a busy week.

The underlying nutritional approach is straightforward: minimize refined carbohydrates and added sugars that spike blood glucose, emphasize protein and healthy fats that keep blood sugar stable, and build in enough calories to maintain energy without triggering hunger-driven slip-ups. This is not a ketogenic plan (carbs are not eliminated entirely) and not a calorie-restriction plan in the traditional sense. It is an insulin-sensitivity plan.

## Calorie and macronutrient targeting

Calculate basal metabolic rate (BMR) using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula. For a man, BMR equals 10 times weight in kilograms, plus 6.25 times height in centimeters, minus 5 times age, plus 5. For a woman, use the same formula but subtract 161 at the end instead of adding 5.

Multiply BMR by the activity multiplier to get maintenance calories (total daily energy expenditure, or TDEE). Use 1.2 for sedentary desk work, 1.375 for light activity a few times a week, 1.55 for moderate regular exercise, 1.725 for physically demanding jobs where someone is on their feet all day (construction, warehouse, nursing), and 1.9 for people combining heavy physical work with daily training.

For A1C management specifically, aim for a daily calorie target that sits about 150 to 250 calories below maintenance. This gentle deficit improves insulin sensitivity without triggering the muscle loss and rebound eating that aggressive cutting produces. Do not go below maintenance minus 500 unless the user has explicitly asked for weight loss as a separate goal, and do not go below 1,800 calories for any adult man or below 1,500 for any adult woman regardless of size.

Macronutrient targets work out roughly as follows. Protein should hit 1.0 gram per pound of body weight, which lands at 160 to 200 grams per day for most adults. Fat fills in the calorie gap and typically runs 40 to 50 percent of total calories. Net carbs (total carbs minus fiber) stay under 50 grams per day, ideally under 35 grams. Sugar stays under 25 grams per day total, with most of that coming from whole fruit rather than added sugar.

These numbers stay behind the scenes. The final document should not show BMR formulas or mention the person's weight. The calorie target drives portion sizing in the meal plan table and the shopping list quantities.

## A Few Things Worth Knowing (diabetic-specific tips)

The following five prose paragraphs should appear in the "A Few Things Worth Knowing" section of any diabetic meal plan. Each has a bolded lead-in followed by explanation. Use these verbatim or adapt the phrasing lightly for the specific person, but keep all five tips and their order.

**Cut out liquid sugar entirely.** Soda, sweet tea, juice, Gatorade, and flavored coffee creamer all hit your bloodstream faster than anything you could eat. Swapping them for water, black coffee, unsweetened tea, or sparkling water is the single biggest thing you can do for your A1C. On hot days when you're sweating through your shirt, grab a zero-sugar electrolyte packet (LMNT or Liquid IV Sugar Free) instead of Gatorade.

**Flip every package before it goes in the cart.** If the sugar content is over 3 grams per serving on any meat, sauce, or marinade, put it back. That one habit catches most of the traps. "Honey BBQ" wings, "teriyaki" anything, and "maple" sausage are all sugar wearing a meat costume.

**Never eat fruit alone.** Pair it with something fatty or with protein: apple with cheese, berries with Greek yogurt, or orange with almonds. The fat and protein slow sugar absorption and flatten the spike. Every meal in this plan already does this; just remember the rule when snacking on your own.

**Real protein at every meal.** Four ounces minimum, more if you're hungry. Protein keeps you full, keeps blood sugar steady through the afternoon, and protects muscle. You'll naturally hit 160 to 200 grams a day on this plan.

**Grab a cheap glucose meter.** They run about 20 dollars at Kroger. Test before you eat and two hours after, for just one week. You'll see exactly which foods spike you and which don't, and that kind of feedback sticks way better than general advice ever does.

## Structural meal pattern

The diabetic plan follows a specific weekly pattern designed to balance variety with simplicity. Breakfasts rotate between four options that cover the week. Lunches are always cold charcuterie boxes assembled the night before, because hot lunches are a hassle for people eating at work or on the road. Dinners use the air fryer as the default cooking method, with one meat-and-salad combination per day, and one built-in ribeye day as a slight upgrade.

A typical week looks like this. Monday dinner: Buffalo wings (repeat on Friday for consistency). Tuesday dinner: bratwurst or Italian sausage. Wednesday dinner: air fryer chicken thighs (cook extras for Friday lunch). Thursday dinner: steak bites with broccoli. Friday dinner: wings again. Saturday dinner: ribeye (treat day). Sunday dinner: rotisserie chicken (doubles as prep for Monday and Tuesday lunch boxes).

This pattern is flexible. If the person dislikes any of these proteins, substitute from the alternatives in the grocery-source file. The key structural principles are: two wing nights (they're easy and reliable), one sausage night, two or three chicken nights, one red meat night, and one rotisserie chicken night that doubles as meal prep.

## Recipe Library

Each recipe below is calibrated for roughly 2,500 calories per day on average. Scale portions proportionally for other calorie targets. Each recipe shows prep time, cook time, total time, and approximate calorie count.

### Breakfasts (four rotating options)

**Scrambled eggs with air fryer bacon.** Prep 2 minutes, cook 8 minutes, total 10 minutes, approximately 620 calories. Lay three strips of bacon in the air fryer basket and cook at 400 degrees for eight minutes. While the bacon cooks, crack four eggs into a bowl and whisk with a pinch of salt. Melt a pat of butter in a skillet over medium heat, pour in the eggs, and stir gently until just set (about three minutes). Plate the eggs, top with bacon, and add half a sliced avocado. The avocado matters for both calorie count and blood sugar steadiness.

**Greek yogurt power bowl.** Prep 2 minutes, no cook time, total 2 minutes, approximately 600 calories. Scoop one and a half cups of Fage 5% Greek yogurt into a bowl. Top with half a cup of fresh berries and one ounce of almonds. Stir in one tablespoon of natural peanut butter for staying power. The full-fat yogurt matters; low-fat versions add sugar to compensate.

**Cheesy eggs with avocado.** Prep 2 minutes, cook 4 minutes, total 6 minutes, approximately 700 calories. Whisk four eggs with salt and pepper. Melt butter in a pan, cook the eggs scrambled, and toss in two torn-up slices of cheddar in the last 30 seconds. Plate with half a sliced avocado and two strips of bacon (optional but recommended). Hot sauce if wanted.

**Cottage cheese with berries.** Prep 1 minute, no cook time, total 1 minute, approximately 520 calories. One and a half cups of Good Culture 4% cottage cheese in a bowl. Top with half a cup of blueberries and one ounce of almonds. A small pinch of salt actually improves the flavor.

### Lunches (three charcuterie box variants)

All assembled the night before in a divided container. Assembly time three to five minutes each, no cook time.

**Classic charcuterie box.** Approximately 860 calories. Four ounces of rolled deli meat, three ounces of cubed cheese, a small handful of olives, cherry tomatoes and cucumber slices, a small handful of spring mix, two or three pepperoncini, one ounce of almonds, and a drizzle of olive oil.

**Rotisserie chicken box.** Approximately 800 calories. Five ounces of rotisserie chicken pulled off the bone, two to three ounces of cheese cubes, cherry tomatoes and cucumber, a handful of olives, and either one ounce of nuts or half an avocado. Optional half of a small apple, eaten with the chicken and cheese.

**Leftover dinner box.** Approximately 850 calories. Whatever protein was left from the previous night (two chicken thighs, five ounces of steak, one bratwurst) paired with cheese, vegetables, olives, and olive oil. The cheat mode of the plan: cook 1.5x dinner, lunch is handled.

### Dinners (one per day of the week)

Each dinner hits roughly 950 to 1,100 calories. All use the air fryer at 400 degrees unless noted.

**Monday: Tyson Buffalo wings.** Prep 1 minute, cook 22 to 25 minutes, approximately 1,050 calories. Dump 10 to 12 frozen wings into the air fryer with no oil needed. Cook at 400 degrees for 22 to 25 minutes, shaking halfway. Serve over bagged salad tossed with olive oil, salt, and pepper, with half a sliced avocado. Confirm the bag says "plain" or "Buffalo" and not Honey BBQ or Teriyaki.

**Tuesday: Johnsonville brats.** Prep 1 minute, cook 12 minutes, approximately 1,060 calories. Prick three brats with a fork to prevent bursting. Cook at 380 degrees for 10 to 12 minutes, flipping halfway. Serve on bagged salad with olive oil, spicy brown mustard, and half an avocado.

**Wednesday: Air fryer chicken thighs.** Prep 3 minutes, cook 16 to 18 minutes, approximately 950 calories. Pat dry three boneless skinless thighs. Cook five total (three for tonight, two saved for Friday's lunch box). Drizzle olive oil, shake on Slap Ya Mama or Tony Chachere's seasoning. Cook at 400 degrees for 16 to 18 minutes, flipping halfway. Serve over salad with olive oil and half an avocado.

**Thursday: Steak bites with broccoli.** Prep 3 minutes, cook 8 minutes, approximately 1,050 calories. Toss 8 to 10 ounces of sirloin bites with olive oil, salt, pepper, and garlic powder. Cook at 400 degrees for 7 to 9 minutes for medium. Microwave a bag of steamable broccoli for four minutes, dump into a bowl with one tablespoon of butter and salt. Plate the steak bites on top with half an avocado.

**Friday: Tyson Buffalo wings (repeat).** Same as Monday. The repeat is deliberate because Friday is usually the longest work day and the person deserves something easy they already know they like.

**Saturday: Air fryer ribeye.** Prep 5 minutes, cook 10 minutes, approximately 1,100 calories. Take the ribeye out of the fridge 30 minutes before cooking. Pat dry, season heavily with salt and pepper, drizzle with olive oil. Cook at 400 degrees for 8 to 10 minutes, flipping halfway. Ten minutes produces medium for a one-inch steak. Rest for five minutes before slicing. Serve with bagged salad and olive oil.

**Sunday: Rotisserie chicken with broccoli.** Prep 2 minutes, cook 4 minutes. Buy the rotisserie chicken on the way home. Microwave steamable broccoli for four minutes, add butter and salt. Eat a drumstick-and-thigh portion worth and shred the remaining chicken into a container for Monday and Tuesday lunch boxes. This dinner doubles as the week's meal prep.

## Scaling for different calorie targets

For roughly 2,000 calories per day, reduce wings from 10 to 12 down to 6 to 8, drop breakfast eggs from 4 to 3, cut lunch cheese from 3 ounces to 2, and remove extra avocado halves from dinners.

For roughly 3,000 calories per day, increase wings to 14 to 16, go to 5 eggs at breakfast, bump lunch cheese to 4 ounces, and add a whole avocado rather than half to each meal. Add a mid-afternoon snack of a hard-boiled egg with an ounce of almonds.

For weight loss combined with diabetes, do not go below maintenance minus 500 calories. Instead of cutting food further, tighten the fruit allowance to every other day and make sure the person is hitting 80-plus ounces of water daily.

## Common allergy and preference substitutions

If the person cannot eat eggs, replace egg breakfasts with Greek yogurt bowls and cottage cheese bowls. The rotation becomes yogurt on Monday and Friday, cottage cheese on Tuesday and Thursday, yogurt again on Saturday, and whatever they prefer on Sunday.

If the person does not like wings, swap in any other air fryer protein: drumsticks, bone-in thighs, or chicken tenders work with identical cook times. The cost per pound on drumsticks is often the lowest.

If the person is lactose intolerant, substitute lactose-free cheese (Cabot makes a line that behaves like regular cheese), skip the Greek yogurt breakfast and double up on egg breakfasts, and use dairy-free butter alternatives (Miyoko's makes a good one).

If the person is vegetarian, this diet type becomes significantly harder because the plan depends heavily on meat-based protein. Substitute eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, and cheese as the main protein sources, and add tempeh or tofu (not beans or legumes, which spike blood sugar) for dinner proteins. The calorie math stays the same but the menu variety is reduced.
